Camera & recording laws

General information, not legal advice. Laws differ by country, state and city and change over time. If you're unsure, check your local rules or ask a lawyer.

Video in and around your own home

  • In most places you may record video on property you own or control, including your yard and the street as seen from it.
  • It's generally illegal to record where people reasonably expect privacy: bathrooms, changing areas, and bedrooms of others without their consent. Many places treat hidden cameras in these areas as a crime.
  • Avoid aiming cameras into neighbours' windows, private yards or homes. Use the camera's privacy masks or reposition it.
  • Renters: check your lease; don't record shared spaces or other tenants without consent. Landlords: generally may not place cameras inside a rented unit.
  • Household workers (nannies, cleaners, carers): some places require you to tell them about cameras; it's good practice everywhere.

Audio is stricter than video

  • Recording a conversation you're not part of can break wiretapping laws.
  • In the US, federal law and most states require at least one party to a conversation to consent. Several states, including California, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Washington, require all parties to consent in many situations.
  • That's why Spectacle never uses a phone's microphone and keeps IP-camera audio off unless an administrator turns it on.

Europe, UK and similar data-protection laws

  • Cameras that only cover your own private property are usually outside the GDPR's scope (the "household exemption").
  • If a camera captures public space, such as the street or a pavement, the GDPR / UK GDPR may apply. Then you should have a good reason, keep footage only as long as needed (Spectacle's retention setting helps), put up a sign, and respond to people who ask for footage of themselves.

Signs

A visible "CCTV / cameras recording" sign is required in some situations and recommended everywhere. It also deters intruders.

Sharing footage

Think before posting footage of identifiable people online. Giving footage to police when a crime is reported is usually fine.

Wildlife

Watching wildlife on your property is fine. Don't disturb nests, bait or trap protected species, or remove protected plants based on what the app logs.
